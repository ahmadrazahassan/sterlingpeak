Every VAT-registered UK business has to submit VAT Returns to HMRC, and since Making Tax Digital for VAT went into full effect, the submission has to be made through MTD-compatible accounting software. This guide walks UK businesses through the full process: from getting the numbers right inside the accounting software, to reviewing the nine boxes on the return, to authorising software with HMRC, to submitting and paying. It covers Sage Accounting, Sage 50, Sage 200, and Sage Intacct at the UK-specific steps that matter.

VAT is one of the most common areas where UK small businesses get penalised. Not because the return itself is complicated, but because the records behind it are messy, the scheme is wrong, or the deadline is missed. Using proper accounting software removes most of that risk — if you use it correctly.

This article is written for UK business owners and bookkeepers preparing their own VAT Returns, and for anyone who wants to understand what their accountant is actually doing each quarter. We focus on the practical workflow rather than the policy.

Who must submit
Every VAT-registered UK business, usually on a quarterly basis under MTD for VAT.
How
Through MTD-compatible accounting software or bridging software that connects to HMRC's VAT API. Manual entry into the old HMRC online form is no longer allowed.
When
One calendar month and seven days after the end of the VAT period. For a quarter ending 31 March, the VAT Return is due by 7 May.
What to submit
A nine-box VAT Return generated automatically by the software from the digital records held inside it.
Payment
By Direct Debit (set up in the HMRC Gateway), BACS, Faster Payments, or CHAPS by the same deadline as submission.
Common mistakes
Wrong VAT scheme, unreconciled bank, missing supplier invoices, reclaiming VAT on disallowed expenses, late submission or late payment.

Before you start: five things that must be in place

Submitting a VAT Return is the last step in a longer process. Get these five things right first and the submission itself takes minutes.

1. Your software must be MTD-compatible

Check that your accounting software appears on HMRC's list of MTD-compatible software for VAT. Sage Accounting, Sage 50, Sage 200, Sage Intacct, Xero, QuickBooks, and FreeAgent are all MTD-compatible. If you are still on an older desktop version that predates MTD, upgrade first.

2. You must be signed up to MTD for VAT with HMRC

Every VAT-registered UK business should already be signed up. If for some reason you are not, sign up on gov.uk using your Government Gateway credentials before you try to file.

3. The correct VAT scheme must be configured

The software needs to know which VAT scheme you are on:

  • Standard VAT accounting — VAT due on invoice date.
  • Cash accounting scheme — VAT due when payment is received/made.
  • Flat rate scheme — a fixed percentage of gross turnover.
  • Annual accounting scheme — single annual return with instalments.
  • Margin schemes — for second-hand goods, antiques, art, etc.

Configure this once, in the software's VAT settings, and every transaction from that point on will use the correct scheme. Changing schemes mid-quarter is rarely a good idea.

4. Your bank account must be reconciled

Every transaction on the bank statement should match a transaction in the accounting software, and the closing balance in the software must match the bank's closing balance. If the bank is not reconciled, the VAT Return is based on incomplete data.

5. All sales and purchase invoices for the period must be entered

The nine-box VAT Return is generated from the invoices in the accounting ledger. Any invoice not yet entered will be missing from the return. A short cut-off checklist:

  • All sales invoices dated within the period are entered and finalised.
  • All purchase invoices dated within the period are entered and finalised.
  • All credit notes are issued and linked to the correct invoices.
  • All expenses (card payments, cash receipts) are recorded.
  • All bank transactions are reconciled.

The UK nine-box VAT Return

Every UK VAT Return uses the same nine boxes. Your software calculates them for you, but you should know what each box means.

Box What it represents
Box 1VAT due on sales and other outputs.
Box 2VAT due on acquisitions from EU member states (if applicable; changed post-Brexit).
Box 3Total VAT due (Box 1 + Box 2).
Box 4VAT reclaimed on purchases and other inputs.
Box 5Net VAT to pay or reclaim (Box 3 − Box 4).
Box 6Total value of sales and other outputs, excluding VAT.
Box 7Total value of purchases and other inputs, excluding VAT.
Box 8Total value of goods supplied to EU member states (post-Brexit this is largely retained for Northern Ireland).
Box 9Total value of acquisitions from EU member states (post-Brexit largely retained for Northern Ireland).

The step-by-step submission process

The steps below apply, with small variations, to every major UK accounting product. Screens and menu labels differ but the sequence is the same.

Step 1: Close the period

Open the VAT module and confirm the period-end date. For quarterly VAT, this is the last day of the VAT quarter (e.g. 31 March, 30 June, 30 September, or 31 December for the most common quarter pattern).

Step 2: Run the draft VAT Return

Generate the draft VAT Return from the software. You should see all nine boxes populated automatically. The draft is a working document until submitted.

Step 3: Review the boxes against expectations

Do not submit without reviewing. Practical checks:

  • Does Box 1 (VAT on sales) look reasonable compared to last quarter?
  • Does Box 4 (VAT reclaimed) look reasonable compared to Box 1?
  • Does Box 6 (total sales excluding VAT) match the sales ledger total for the quarter?
  • Does Box 7 (total purchases excluding VAT) match the purchase ledger total for the quarter?
  • Is there any number that is dramatically higher or lower than the previous quarter? Investigate the reason.

Step 4: Drill into any unusual figures

Every good accounting product lets you click from a box on the VAT Return down to the individual transactions that make it up. Use this. If Box 4 looks suspiciously high, drill into purchases and check for an invoice in the wrong period, a duplicate, or a transaction where VAT has been claimed on a non-business expense.

Step 5: Make corrections in the ledger, not in the return

If something is wrong, correct it in the underlying invoice, bill, or journal. Then re-run the draft VAT Return. Never override the numbers on the return itself — that breaks the digital link that MTD requires.

Step 6: Authorise the software with HMRC

The first time you submit from any piece of software, HMRC will ask you to grant authority. The authority lasts 18 months, after which it can be renewed. You can check or withdraw permissions at any time via your HMRC Government Gateway account.

Step 7: Submit the return

Click submit (or "File" or "Submit to HMRC" — the wording varies). The software sends the nine-box data to HMRC's VAT API and returns a receipt reference. Save that reference; it is the confirmation that HMRC received the return.

Step 8: Pay the VAT

Payment is due by the same deadline as the submission (one month and seven days after the period end). Options:

  • Direct Debit — set up in the HMRC Gateway; HMRC collects automatically three working days after the deadline.
  • Faster Payments — same-day transfer.
  • BACS — takes three working days; plan ahead.
  • CHAPS — same-day, higher-value option.
  • Card payment — via HMRC's online payment service.

Direct Debit is the safest option for most UK businesses. It removes the risk of forgetting to pay, which carries interest and penalty charges. Set it up once in the HMRC Gateway and leave it running.

How Sage products handle VAT submission

Sage's UK products all use the same principles, with slightly different interfaces:

Sage Accounting

VAT Return generation is a core feature across Start, Standard, and Plus tiers. Navigate to the VAT module, choose the period, review the draft, drill into transactions, and submit directly to HMRC from within the product. Sage Copilot can answer natural-language questions about the return figures.

Sage 50

Sage 50 has mature VAT return workflows. The VAT Return wizard walks through period selection, reconciliation of unreconciled transactions, and submission to HMRC through the built-in MTD module.

Sage 200

Sage 200 VAT handling is more elaborate because the ledger is deeper. VAT codes are applied at transaction entry, the nine-box return is generated from posted ledgers, and submission is made through the MTD module. Partial exemption and multi-rate businesses are supported.

Sage Intacct

Sage Intacct supports UK VAT including standard, reduced, zero, exempt, and outside-scope rates, plus partial exemption, flat-rate scheme, and the domestic reverse charge for construction (CIS). Submission to HMRC is made either through direct API integration or an approved bridging route, depending on partner setup.

VAT schemes: pick the right one

The scheme you are on affects how the software calculates the return.

Scheme How VAT is calculated Best for
Standard VAT due on invoice date (output and input). Most UK businesses with straightforward invoicing.
Cash accounting VAT due when payment is received/made. UK small businesses with taxable turnover under £1.35m who want to smooth cash flow.
Flat rate A fixed percentage of gross VAT-inclusive turnover. Small UK businesses with low expenses; simpler admin, generally cannot reclaim VAT on purchases.
Annual accounting One return per year with interim payments on account. Businesses wanting to reduce administrative frequency.
Margin scheme VAT charged on the margin (sale price − purchase price) for eligible goods. Second-hand goods, antiques, art, collectibles.

Choosing the wrong scheme is a common mistake. If you are uncertain, ask your accountant. Changing schemes requires HMRC agreement and should not be done mid-quarter.

Common UK VAT return errors

  • Reclaiming VAT on non-business expenses — personal use should be excluded.
  • Reclaiming VAT on entertainment — client entertainment is generally not reclaimable; staff entertainment is limited.
  • Reclaiming VAT on fuel without keeping proper mileage records — use the HMRC scale charge or the actual business-use basis consistently.
  • Charging the wrong VAT rate — check zero-rated vs exempt vs standard for the specific goods or services.
  • Missing reverse charge for construction (CIS) — the domestic reverse charge applies to certain construction services between VAT-registered businesses.
  • Applying VAT on outside-the-scope supplies — donations, dividends, and some insurance payouts are outside VAT entirely.
  • Forgetting to adjust for bad debts — VAT can be reclaimed on bad debts that meet HMRC's conditions.
  • Mixing gross and net amounts — in spreadsheet-based processes; software avoids this by forcing VAT codes.

Late submissions and penalties

HMRC moved to a points-based penalty regime for VAT in 2023. Late submissions accumulate points; once a threshold is reached, a £200 penalty is charged for every subsequent late submission. Points expire after a period of compliant filing. Late payment penalties apply separately, calculated as a percentage of the unpaid VAT, with interest also charged from the original due date.

The practical rule: file on time, pay on time. Set up a Direct Debit to remove the payment risk, and put a calendar reminder in place two weeks before each VAT deadline.

A reusable monthly/quarterly VAT checklist

Most UK businesses benefit from a standard checklist. Run through this every month if you are on annual accounting, and every quarter otherwise:

  • All sales invoices for the period are raised and posted.
  • All purchase invoices for the period are entered and coded.
  • All expense receipts are captured (card payments, cash, staff claims).
  • Bank accounts are fully reconciled to statement.
  • Credit cards and merchant accounts are reconciled.
  • Any payroll journals are posted.
  • Recurring invoices and standing journals are up to date.
  • VAT-eligible expense categories (motor, subsistence, entertainment) are reviewed.
  • CIS reverse charge entries (construction) are reviewed.
  • Draft VAT Return run and reviewed against prior period.
  • Any anomalies drilled into and corrected in the ledger.
  • VAT Return submitted via MTD software before deadline.
  • VAT paid via Direct Debit, Faster Payments, or CHAPS before deadline.
  • Submission receipt saved with the quarter's records.

Key points to remember

UK VAT submission in 2026 is a reliable process if the underlying records are clean and the software is set up correctly. The real work is done throughout the quarter — entering invoices, reconciling the bank, applying consistent VAT codes — not in the last hour before submission.

Use MTD-compatible accounting software, keep the scheme configured correctly, reconcile every bank account every month, review each draft VAT Return against the prior quarter, and pay by Direct Debit. Follow that routine and VAT is a small, predictable part of running a UK business rather than a quarterly fire drill.

If the process still feels fragile, it is usually a sign that either the software is wrong for the business, the scheme is misconfigured, or the bookkeeping discipline behind it needs tightening up. Fix the cause, not the VAT Return itself.

Submitting a correction after the fact

Occasionally a submitted VAT Return turns out to be wrong. The rules depend on the size of the error.

  • Small errors (net value under the HMRC threshold and not deliberate) can be adjusted on the next VAT Return. The accounting software's VAT adjustment function handles this; document the reason in the supporting notes.
  • Larger errors or deliberate ones must be disclosed to HMRC formally using the VAT error correction process (VAT652 or the online equivalent). Interest may apply; penalties depend on behaviour.

Do not repeatedly adjust VAT on successive returns to "fix" previous returns. It looks like error stacking to HMRC and attracts review. One clean correction is always preferable to three small adjustments across quarters.

Tips for a painless UK VAT quarter

A few practical habits that UK bookkeepers rely on to keep VAT quarters uneventful:

  • Reconcile every bank account weekly, not at quarter-end.
  • Capture supplier invoices as they arrive, not in a batch at month-end.
  • Use bank rules in the accounting software for recurring transactions.
  • Use document capture tools (Dext, AutoEntry, Hubdoc) to get supplier bills into the ledger quickly.
  • Close each month's VAT-related transactions before the next month starts.
  • Review the VAT liability account monthly for unusual balances.
  • Keep a short "VAT notes" document recording any unusual treatments, rulings, or adjustments during the quarter.
  • Set reminders 10 days and 2 days before the submission deadline.
  • Run the draft VAT Return at least a week before the deadline so there is time to investigate anomalies.
  • Keep the accountant in the loop on anything unusual — changes of scheme, new types of supply, cross-border transactions.

Training and handover inside the business

VAT submission is often owned by one person in a UK small business. That is a risk. If that person leaves or is ill, someone else has to run the quarter. Training a secondary user who understands the VAT workflow — even at a light touch — is worth the time. Most accounting software lets you add additional users with VAT permissions; use this to document the process, not just to provide cover.

A short internal handover document covering the VAT scheme in use, the deadline cadence, the software submission process, the Direct Debit setup, and the accountant's contact details makes the whole process resilient.

How Making Tax Digital changes the submission workflow

Before Making Tax Digital, UK businesses could type the nine boxes directly into HMRC's online VAT return form. That is no longer allowed. Under MTD, the submission must happen through software that holds the underlying digital records and transmits them through HMRC's VAT API.

What this means practically:

  • Records must be kept in a digital format — accounting software or a spreadsheet plus bridging tool.
  • The link between records and the submitted return must be digital — no manual typing in between.
  • HMRC's old VAT online filing form is closed to most UK businesses.
  • Corrections must also flow through compatible software; they cannot be typed into HMRC's web form.
  • The authorisation your software has with HMRC lasts 18 months and can be renewed.

This shift is permanent. UK businesses still running on paper ledgers or pure spreadsheets without bridging tools are out of compliance.

Quarterly vs annual vs monthly VAT periods

Most UK VAT-registered businesses submit quarterly. Some options exist:

  • Quarterly (standard) — four VAT Returns per year. The default.
  • Monthly — twelve returns per year. Used mainly by businesses in repayment position (reclaiming more VAT than they charge, e.g. exporters or zero-rated suppliers) to receive repayments faster.
  • Annual accounting scheme — one annual return with interim payments on account. Reduces admin frequency but requires reconciling payments on account at year-end.

Changing VAT period frequency requires HMRC agreement and is normally a good conversation to have with your accountant before requesting.

What HMRC actually checks

HMRC's VAT compliance activity varies, but the common checks you can expect during a review include:

  • Consistency between Box 6 and your declared turnover on other tax returns.
  • Ratio of VAT reclaimed (Box 4) to VAT charged (Box 1) — anomalies get flagged.
  • Supporting documentation for every item over a materiality threshold.
  • Proper VAT invoices on all reclaimed purchases.
  • Correct VAT treatment of cross-border supplies (post-Brexit rules).
  • Proper CIS reverse charge treatment for UK construction businesses.
  • Evidence of partial exemption calculations where applicable.

Accounting software produces much of this automatically if set up correctly. The key is that the records are genuine and complete, not just that the software is modern.

When to get professional help

Most UK small businesses can handle VAT submission themselves once the software is set up. Situations where professional help is worth the fee:

  • First VAT Return after registration — get the setup right from day one.
  • Moving between VAT schemes.
  • Partial exemption calculations.
  • Cross-border VAT, post-Brexit imports, EU Northern Ireland protocol.
  • CIS reverse charge in construction.
  • Margin schemes (second-hand goods, antiques, art).
  • Any HMRC investigation, even a routine check.
  • Error corrections above the HMRC threshold.

A good UK accountant charges far less for this kind of targeted help than the penalty cost of getting it wrong. Know what you can handle and what you should outsource.
