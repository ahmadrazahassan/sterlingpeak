MTD for Income Tax starts on 6 April 2026 for sole traders and landlords with qualifying income above £50,000. If you need MTD-compatible software for 2026, this guide walks through the products UK businesses are realistically choosing, what "compatible" actually means, and how to pick the right one for your situation.

The words "MTD-compatible" cover a lot of ground. HMRC maintains official lists of software that works with both MTD for VAT and MTD for Income Tax, but being on the list is only the starting point. The software also has to fit your business — sole trader versus limited company, VAT scheme, industry, transaction volume, payroll needs, and what your accountant supports.

This article gives you the regulatory context you need, the criteria that actually matter, and the shortlist of UK products worth evaluating. It is written for UK businesses making a real decision, not for people curious about the policy.

What Making Tax Digital actually requires

HMRC's Making Tax Digital programme has two established strands (VAT and Income Tax) with Corporation Tax expected later. The core requirements across both strands are the same in principle:

  • Digital records. Income and expenses must be kept in a digital format, not in paper ledgers.
  • Digital links. Data must flow between systems digitally, without manual re-typing between the record-keeping stage and submission.
  • Compatible software. Submissions to HMRC must be made through HMRC-approved software that connects to HMRC's APIs.
  • Quarterly updates (Income Tax). For MTD for Income Tax, four quarterly updates per tax year plus a final declaration.
  • Penalties. A points-based penalty regime applies, with a soft landing period at rollout.

HMRC publishes official lists of compatible software on gov.uk, one for MTD for VAT and one for MTD for Income Tax. The lists are regularly updated as vendors complete their certifications.

MTD for VAT — the current rules

All VAT-registered UK businesses must use compatible software to keep VAT records and file VAT Returns. The HMRC guidance is clear: there is either a compatible software package that keeps digital records and submits VAT Returns, or bridging software that connects non-compatible software (typically a spreadsheet) to HMRC systems. Manual entry into HMRC's online VAT return form is no longer permitted for UK VAT-registered businesses.

In practice this means:

  • Sales and purchase records must be captured in digital form.
  • The VAT return must be prepared from those digital records without manual re-keying.
  • Submission to HMRC must happen through software that talks to HMRC's VAT API.
  • When software grants authority to HMRC, the authority lasts 18 months and can be checked or withdrawn at any time.

MTD for Income Tax — the 2026 change

MTD for Income Tax is the most significant UK tax admin change since MTD for VAT. The rollout is staged:

  • 6 April 2026 — sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000 must start using MTD for Income Tax.
  • 6 April 2027 — the threshold drops (watch HMRC publications for the final figure).
  • 6 April 2028 — further rollout to smaller sole traders and landlords.

For the 2026 rollout, HMRC has confirmed a soft landing: penalty points will not be applied for late quarterly updates in the first tax year (2026 to 2027), though late final declarations and late tax payments will still carry penalties.

To use MTD for Income Tax you must be registered for Self Assessment and have submitted a tax return in the last two years. You sign up on gov.uk with the user ID and password you used for Self Assessment. Agents can sign up clients on their behalf.

Action for sole traders and landlords: if you expect qualifying income above £50,000 for the 2026 to 2027 tax year, you should choose MTD-compatible software now and consider signing up voluntarily before the April 2026 deadline so you have a clean setup in time.

Criteria for picking MTD-compatible software

Being "on the HMRC list" is a minimum. Real UK businesses need more than that. Use the following criteria to evaluate MTD-compatible products.

  • HMRC certification scope. Is the product certified for MTD for VAT, MTD for Income Tax, or both?
  • Business type fit. Is it designed for sole traders, landlords, limited companies, or all three?
  • VAT scheme support. Does it support standard, cash, flat-rate, and margin schemes if you use them?
  • Bank feed coverage. Does it connect to your UK bank automatically for transaction import?
  • Accountant support. Is it widely supported by UK accountants and bookkeepers?
  • Payroll integration. If you need payroll, does the product pair cleanly with MTD-compatible payroll?
  • Growth path. Does the product have higher tiers you can grow into, or are you limited by its ceiling?
  • UK pricing clarity. Is pricing published in GBP with clear tiers?
  • Mobile support. Can you raise invoices and capture expenses on a phone?
  • Customer support. Is UK-based support included, and at what hours?

Bridging software — when spreadsheets are still part of the picture

HMRC allows bridging software as a valid MTD route. Bridging software connects a spreadsheet (or other non-compatible software) to HMRC via the MTD API, maintaining the digital link. This is a genuine option for businesses where finance processes are still spreadsheet-based.

Common UK-focused bridging tools include:

  • Sage's bridging solutions within its product family.
  • Tax Systems and other specialist UK bridging products.
  • Vitaltax, 123Sheets, and Absolute Excel VAT.

Bridging software works, but it rarely reduces complexity. For most UK SMEs, moving directly to proper MTD-compatible accounting software is simpler and cheaper over time than maintaining a spreadsheet-plus-bridging stack. Bridging is most justifiable for businesses with unusual reporting requirements, bespoke internal systems, or transition scenarios during a migration.

Common UK MTD mistakes

  • Assuming any accounting software is MTD-compatible — always check HMRC's published list.
  • Leaving the move too late and trying to migrate during a busy tax quarter.
  • Picking software that does not fit the accountant's practice, creating handoff friction.
  • Choosing a bridging tool as a permanent solution when proper software would be cheaper overall.
  • Ignoring the £50,000 threshold for MTD for Income Tax and missing the April 2026 start.
  • Signing up multiple MTD licences by accident — one for VAT, another for Income Tax, when one product covers both.

Setting up MTD on a new product

Once you have chosen MTD-compatible software, the setup itself is straightforward but must be done in the right order. The sequence below applies to any MTD for VAT product.

Step 1: configure the business profile

Enter the company name, VAT registration number, VAT scheme, VAT periods, and company address. Double-check the VAT number because an error here will cause submissions to fail.

Step 2: connect the product to HMRC

Inside the VAT module the software will prompt you to grant authority to HMRC via the Government Gateway. Sign in with your business's Government Gateway credentials, and authorise the software to submit on your behalf. The authority lasts 18 months.

Step 3: import or enter opening balances

Set the first period as the one starting immediately after your last submitted VAT Return. Opening balances for debtors, creditors, VAT control, and bank should match the position at that date. Ask your accountant if there is any doubt.

Step 4: configure VAT codes

Make sure every VAT rate your business uses is configured: standard (20%), reduced (5%), zero-rated, exempt, outside the scope, and any margin or reverse-charge codes you need. Map them to the default nominal accounts.

Step 5: run a test submission

Generate a draft VAT Return for the current period (even if incomplete) and walk through the submission screens without actually submitting. Confirm the software can reach HMRC and retrieve open obligations. This is the equivalent of a dry-run.

Step 6: set up Direct Debit

Direct Debit for VAT is set up in the HMRC Gateway, not in your accounting software. Once set up, HMRC collects the VAT due three working days after the submission deadline, which removes the risk of forgetting to pay.

What to do when software says "not compatible"

Occasionally HMRC's MTD API rejects a submission with an error about compatibility or authorisation. The usual causes and fixes:

  • Authority expired — renew via the software's reconnect flow.
  • Wrong VAT number — double-check the registration number format.
  • Wrong VAT period selected — match the period shown in HMRC's open obligations.
  • Scheme mismatch — software configured for standard VAT while HMRC has you on flat rate (or vice versa). Reconcile.
  • Gateway credentials wrong — sign in directly on HMRC first to verify the credentials work.

Most UK MTD issues boil down to one of those five causes. The vendor's support team is the first port of call; HMRC's VAT helpline can help with scheme and obligation questions but will not debug the software.

Looking ahead: what to expect after 2026

Making Tax Digital will continue to evolve across the second half of the decade. UK businesses that are adopting MTD-compatible software now should also plan for:

  • MTD for Income Tax threshold drops. The £50,000 threshold from April 2026 is the first step; from April 2027 the threshold lowers and from April 2028 the rollout expands further down the market.
  • MTD for Corporation Tax. HMRC has signalled intent but has not yet confirmed a final rollout date. Limited companies should pick MTD-ready software with strong compliance pedigree.
  • Wider digital links requirements. HMRC expects the chain from record-keeping to submission to stay digital. Expect stricter enforcement.
  • Greater API use. HMRC continues to expand the scope of data exchanged via API, which only works with proper MTD-compatible software.

Future-proofing against the direction of travel is a real consideration for UK businesses. Choosing software that is already on HMRC's compatible-software list and that has a track record of supporting new MTD obligations as they roll out is the safer bet over a 3–5 year horizon.

How accountants choose MTD software for UK clients

UK accountants and bookkeepers tend to standardise on one or two MTD-compatible platforms across their client base. The reasons are practical:

  • Training overhead is lower if the whole practice is on the same stack.
  • Client handover is simpler inside a standardised product.
  • Practice management tools (like Sage for Accountants) integrate best with their matching small business product.
  • Fee quoting is easier because the time commitment is predictable.
  • Quality control is simpler when every client file has the same structure.

What this means for UK business owners: your accountant's preference carries weight. If your accountant runs a Sage-first practice, moving to Xero is cheap for you but expensive for them, and the fee picture usually follows. Align the software with the practice unless there is a specific reason not to.
